How These Differ

One Governs AI You Already Run. One Puts It to Work. One Decides Where It May Run at All. One Shows What It Costs.

They get confused for each other because all four have "AI" in the name. They are not variants of one offering and they do not serve the same buyer.

🛡

OcaaS is oversight

The AI already exists and is making decisions inside your organization. OcaaS is the function that watches it, documents it, and reviews what changes. The buyer is usually legal, compliance, or a CTO answering to a board.

📞

Automation is capability

The work is being missed because there is no one to do it at 9pm on a Saturday. Automation is the system that does it. The buyer is usually the owner, and the pain is a phone that rang and nobody answered.

🗂

AI Implementation is the decision underneath both

Nothing has been deployed yet, and the question is not which product to buy — it is which data may touch which system, in what order, and who signs off. The buyer is usually an owner or executive who has been asked to approve something they cannot yet evaluate.

💳

AI Spend & Exposure Assessment is visibility

The AI is already in use and it bills by usage, so the cost follows whoever is using it. The assessment shows what is being spent, who owns it, and how keys are handled — then hands over the dashboard, alerting, and policy. It is delivered through Technology Advisory. The buyer is usually the CFO, controller, or IT lead who just got a surprise AI invoice — or doesn't want to.
