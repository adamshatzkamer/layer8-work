The Assessment

A Three-Section Rubric Built for Underwriters.

Not a generic security framework. Calibrated to the actual criteria carriers evaluate — including the AI sections most assessments don't touch.

🔒

Section 1 — Universal Cyber Hygiene

Baseline controls every carrier expects regardless of vertical or AI exposure: MFA enforcement, endpoint protection, patch management, backup architecture, incident response documentation, and privileged access controls.

Required for any tier of insurability
Scored 0–2 per item, weighted by carrier priority
🤖

Section 2 — AI Exposure & Governance

The section your questionnaire now includes and most firms can't answer with evidence. AI tool inventory, acceptable-use policy, training and data governance, vendor AI risk, output validation, AI-specific incident response, and red-team posture.

Covers ISO AI exclusions CG 40 47, CG 40 48, CG 35 08
Maps to carrier-specific AI Riders
⚖️

Section 3 — Vertical Compliance Overlay

Vertical-specific requirements layered on top of the baseline. Healthcare maps HIPAA and ePHI-AI interaction controls. Legal maps ABA Model Rules 1.6 and 5.3, privilege risk, and client data governance. More overlays in development.

Healthcare operational now
Legal, insurance, accounting, real estate in development
📊

Scoring → Insurability Tier

Each item scored 0–2. Section weights applied. Total maps to four tiers: Insurable / Conditional / At Risk / Likely Denial. Downgrade rules apply when any single section falls below threshold — a strong Section 1 doesn't rescue a failing Section 2.

The Differentiator

Carriers Aren't Converging. They're Diverging.

Generic "AI exclusions are coming" assessments give you the same output regardless of who your carrier is. That's not useful when carriers are moving in five different directions.

CIPU is calibrated to your carrier's actual position. The report names which pattern applies to your carrier and tailors recommendations accordingly — so the remediation priorities in your report are the ones that matter for your renewal, not someone else's.

Affirmative Expansion

Coalition, Embroker, AXA XL — explicitly covering AI losses; stricter questionnaires with higher evidence bar.

Sublimits

Beazley, QBE — AI losses covered but sublimited; posture documentation influences sublimit negotiation.

Exclusion

AIG, Hamilton — AI losses excluded by endorsement; report identifies what's at risk under current terms.

Underwriting Tightening

Westfield — baseline controls requirements are stricter; Section 1 posture is a renewal gating factor.

Acquisition / Consolidation

Zurich/Beazley — position is still resolving; report flags what to clarify with your broker before submission.

Questions

Common Questions.

Is this the same as a SOC 2 assessment? +

No. SOC 2 is built for vendor diligence — it tells your customers that your controls are reliable. CIPU is built for the underwriting moment — it tells your carrier that the controls you attested to on your application actually exist and are correctly configured. Different output shape, different audience, different stakes.

My renewal is in six weeks. Is there time? +

Possibly. The assessment takes 2–3 weeks for most organizations in the target range. We can start within days of a signed engagement. Call us now — the scoping conversation is 30 minutes and free. If the timeline doesn't work, we'll tell you on the call.

We already have a GRC platform (Vanta, Drata, Secureframe). Do we need this? +

Those platforms produce outputs shaped for sales and vendor diligence — they help you win customers, not satisfy underwriters. The artifact they produce doesn't map to the ISO AI exclusions or carrier-specific AI Riders your questionnaire now references. CIPU produces a different output, for a different moment, built to a different standard.
